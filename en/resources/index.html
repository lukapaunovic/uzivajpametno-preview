What the guidelines say about PrEP

PrEP is preventive medication for people who do not have HIV. When taken as prescribed, it reduces the risk of acquiring HIV through sex by more than 99%. An HIV test and a medical assessment are needed before starting. With oral PrEP, regular HIV testing and medical reviews usually take place every three months. PrEP does not protect against other sexually transmitted infections.

  • WHO: Implementation tool for PrEP, provider module : international recommendations for providing PrEP services, 2024 edition.
  • EACS Guidelines : European clinical guidelines; version 13.0 was available at the time of review.

The dosing regimen depends on the medication, your health and sexual practices. Your doctor helps you choose an appropriate regimen. International guidelines also cover options that may not be available in Serbia.

Experience with PrEP and safe use

Information about tolerability, dosing regimens, testing and medical reviews is based on the second edition of POTENT's brochure, pages 5-9 and 14-19. It also describes rare side effects, limitations and the risks of obtaining medication without professional support.

The approval date comes from the FDA document on Truvada for PrEP (PDF): the medication was approved for PrEP in the US on 16 July 2012. This is not the date PrEP was introduced in Serbia.

The statement about millions of users is supported by the UNAIDS Global AIDS Update 2025 (PDF): around 3.9 million people used PrEP during 2024. This figure refers to people who used it during the year, not necessarily every day.

Equal effectiveness of daily and on-demand regimens refers to correct use in groups for whom the regimen is appropriate. The doctor assesses the type of exposure, health conditions, other medications and the need for additional monitoring. Hepatitis B alone does not mean PrEP can never be used; it requires a plan for taking and, if necessary, stopping it. An HIV test and consultation every three months do not mean that every laboratory test is repeated at that interval.

Planned online PrEP service

Play Safe provides a route to online PrEP support from Potent Extra Care through Apoteka.rs. A single link leads to a partner PrEP page, where users are expected to choose between starting or continuing PrEP.

The planned process includes a relevant questionnaire on Apoteka.rs, an online consultation, tests as instructed by the doctor and submission of results through the platform. After reviewing the results, the doctor decides whether to prescribe PrEP. Prescription fulfilment and delivery options depend on the partner service's terms.

If possible exposure has already happened

PEP is emergency prevention after possible exposure to HIV. Seek medical assessment immediately and start treatment within 72 hours at the latest. The sooner, the better. Do not wait for a routine PrEP appointment.
